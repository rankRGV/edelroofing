Commercial roofs work differently from residential ones. Most are flat or low slope, covered with a membrane system instead of shingles, and a failure can shut down a business. This guide covers how to tell your commercial roof in Edinburg needs attention, what repair or replacement costs depend on, and what to expect when you call a contractor.

## What is considered commercial roofing?

Commercial roofing is roof work on buildings used for business: offices, retail strip centers, warehouses, medical buildings, churches and apartment complexes. Most commercial roofs are flat or low slope and use a membrane system (TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen) instead of shingles. Repairing, recoating or replacing one takes different materials, equipment and scheduling than a house does.

## How do you know your commercial roof needs flat roof repair?

Look for these signs:

- **Standing water after rain.** A flat roof should drain within 24 to 48 hours. Water that sits longer points to a drainage problem or a low spot in the roof.
- **Bubbles or blisters in the membrane.** This usually means moisture got under the roofing material.
- **Cracks or splits in the membrane.** Sun and heat cycles crack membranes over time, especially at the seams and edges. Hailstorms across Hidalgo County add impact damage on top of normal wear.
- **Leaks inside or water stains on ceiling tiles.** By the time water shows up inside, it has usually been getting in for a while.
- **Higher energy bills.** A roof that has lost its reflective coating soaks up more heat, so your air conditioning works harder.
- **The roof is more than 15 years old.** Most commercial membrane systems in the RGV last 15 to 25 years, depending on the material and maintenance.

If you see any of these, have the roof inspected instead of guessing.

## What is the most common type of commercial roof?

The most common type of commercial roof today is TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) membrane. It's a white, reflective system that handles heat well and costs less than the other options. In Edinburg and across Hidalgo County, the strip centers along Closner and McColl, the warehouses, and the medical and professional offices near DHR Health and UTRGV are almost all flat or low slope. So the same three systems show up again and again:

- **TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin).** The white, reflective membrane described above, and the default choice for new commercial roofs today.
- **EPDM (rubber roofing).** A durable, flexible membrane that has been used on commercial buildings for decades. A good pick when budget and lifespan both matter.
- **Modified bitumen.** A multilayer system made from asphalt, often used on smaller commercial buildings and additions.

The right system for your building depends on the roof's size, slope and existing structure, and on your budget. A contractor should walk you through the options instead of quoting you only one.

## Repair, recoat, or replace?

A commercial roof that's showing problems has three options, and the right one depends on its condition:

- **Repair.** Makes sense for isolated problems like a small leak, a damaged seam or one section of membrane. It's the lower cost choice when the rest of the roof is sound.
- **Recoat (roof coating).** A reflective coating applied over a membrane that is still structurally sound but worn. It extends the life of the roof for a fraction of what replacement costs.
- **Replace.** Needed when the membrane has reached the end of its life, when leaks are widespread, or when the decking underneath has water damage.

A roofer should inspect the whole roof, and not only the problem area, before recommending one of these.

## What is the 25% roof rule?

Many building codes follow a 25% rule for reroofing. If more than 25% of a roof's surface needs repair or replacement within a 12 month period, code typically requires the entire roof, or that full section, to be brought up to current code. This matters for your budget, because a repair that looks small can turn into a much bigger job once it crosses that line. An inspection tells you where your roof stands before you commit to a repair plan.
