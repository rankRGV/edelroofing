How long a replacement takes, whether insurance covers it, and what to expect in McAllen

Once a McAllen homeowner decides a roof replacement is the right call, two questions come up almost every time: how long is this going to take, and is any of it covered by insurance? Both answers depend on specifics, but there are real ranges worth knowing before a crew shows up.

How long does a roof replacement take in McAllen?

Most residential replacements in McAllen take one to three days from tear-off to cleanup, weather permitting. Where a specific job falls in that range comes down to a few things:

  • Roof size and pitch. A single-story home with a straightforward gable roof moves faster than a steep, multi-level roof with valleys and dormers.
  • Decking condition. If the tear-off reveals water-damaged decking, that section has to be repaired or replaced before the new roof goes on — this is the most common reason a one-day job becomes a two-day job.
  • Material. Standard asphalt shingle replacement is faster than a switch to metal, which involves more custom flashing work.
  • Weather. Rain delays a replacement outright — a torn-off roof cannot sit exposed when a storm rolls in. In McAllen's summer rainy stretches, a job can shift a day or two around the forecast.

New construction and larger homes in the newer subdivisions around Sharyland and Las Cumbres can run toward the longer end simply because the roofs are bigger. Older homes near 2nd Street and 10th Street are often smaller footprints, but if the decking underneath has been quietly absorbing moisture for a decade, that is where the timeline stretches.

Does insurance cover a roof replacement in McAllen?

Sometimes, and it depends entirely on the cause. Homeowners insurance generally covers roof replacement when the damage comes from a sudden, specific event — a hailstorm, high winds, a fallen tree limb. It does not cover a roof that is simply old and worn out from years of heat and UV exposure. Insurers treat that as a maintenance issue, not a covered loss.

That distinction matters a lot in McAllen. The newer construction around Sharyland and Las Cumbres is more likely to see a storm-driven claim — a hail event damages a roof that still had plenty of service life left, and insurance picks up some or all of the replacement. The older stock near 2nd Street and 10th Street is more often past its expected lifespan from age alone, which means a claim is less likely to hold up even if the roof clearly needs replacing.

What to expect, start to finish

  1. Inspection. We walk the full roof and check the decking, not just the section that looks bad from the ground. If storm damage might be involved, we document it in writing.
  2. Written estimate. You get a specific number and a specific timeline before any work begins — no surprise charges once the crew is on the roof.
  3. Tear-off. The old roofing system comes off down to the decking. This is when hidden problems — soft spots, water damage — actually get found.
  4. Decking repair, if needed. Any damaged decking gets replaced before the new roof goes on. This step is what most often changes a one-day estimate into two.
  5. Installation. New underlayment and shingle system installed to manufacturer spec.
  6. Cleanup. Debris removed, magnetic sweep for loose nails, site left clean.

Frequently asked questions about roof replacement timing and insurance in McAllen

Can a roof replacement happen in one day?

Sometimes, on smaller, simpler roofs with no decking issues. But most McAllen homes fall into the one-to-three-day range once you account for roof size and the possibility of decking repair. We give you a specific estimate after inspecting, not a generic "one day" promise.

Will my insurance company cover a full replacement or just part of it?

It depends on your policy and the cause of the damage. Some policies cover full replacement cost, others depreciate based on the roof's age (actual cash value). We cannot tell you what your specific policy covers — that is a conversation with your insurance company — but we can document the roof's condition so you have accurate information going into that conversation.

What if the tear-off finds damage insurance did not approve for?

This happens. If we find decking damage during tear-off that was not part of the original claim, we document it and let you know before doing the work, so you can go back to your adjuster with photos and a written finding if needed.

Yes. Older roofs — especially the 1980s and 90s stock common near 2nd Street and 10th Street — are more likely to need decking repair once the old shingles come off, which is the main variable that extends a job. Newer homes in areas like Sharyland and Las Cumbres usually move faster unless there's storm damage involved.
