What roofing problems are most common in Mission?

The older parts of Mission, around Conway Avenue and the streets running off it toward downtown, have a lot of homes with shingle roofs that have been through more than one Valley summer than they were rated for. On those we are usually looking at full replacement rather than repair, because patching a roof that is already past its service life tends to buy you one season and not much more.

Out in the Sharyland area and the newer subdivisions west and north of town, the roofs are younger and the work is different. There we see more storm damage, more flashing and vent issues, and more homes where a targeted repair is genuinely the right call. We would rather do the repair than sell you a replacement you do not need yet.

On the commercial side, the retail along Expressway 83 and the businesses near Mission Regional Medical Center mostly have flat or low-slope roofs. Flat roofs fail differently than pitched roofs. Water sits instead of running off, so a small problem stays wet and spreads, and by the time it shows up on a ceiling tile the actual breach can be a good distance away. Finding the real source is most of the job.

One thing that applies to every roof in Mission: the heat here is the main enemy. UV exposure and sustained high temperatures break shingles down faster in the Valley than almost anywhere else in the country. A roof that would last 25 years somewhere cooler will not last that long here. Add hail a few times a decade and the case for an inspection after any real storm is easy to make.

Do I need a full replacement or can my roof be repaired?

It depends on the age of the roof and how widespread the damage is. If the shingles are near the end of their service life, a repair usually buys you a season and you end up replacing it anyway. If the roof is younger and the damage is in one area, a repair is the right call.
